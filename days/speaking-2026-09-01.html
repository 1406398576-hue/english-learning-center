The Dentist I Almost Didn't Go To

那个我差点没勇气走进去的牙医诊所
"The worst part wasn't the drill. It was the three months I spent pretending the pain wasn't real."
📅 Day 35 · 2026-09-01 🎯 B1–B2 🏷 People & Society

B Short Story · 短故事

Lena had been ignoring the ache in her back tooth since June. It started as a small sensitivity to cold water, then became a dull pressure she could feel when she lay down at night. By August, she was chewing only on the left side of her mouth and avoiding ice cream entirely. She told herself it was stress. That it would go away. That dentists were expensive and dramatic and always found something wrong.

The truth was simpler: she was scared. As a child, she had a dentist who never explained anything, who moved too fast and talked too little. The sound of the drill had stayed in her body like a memory she couldn't shake.

On the first Saturday of September, the pain woke her up at 4 a.m. She lay in the dark, hand pressed against her cheek, and finally opened her phone. She found a small clinic two blocks away with a photo of a smiling woman in blue scrubs. The reviews mentioned the same word over and over: gentle.

Dr. Park was younger than Lena expected. She didn't rush. She showed Lena the X-ray, pointed to the dark spot, and said, "This is a cavity. It's deep, but it's fixable. The sooner we do it, the better." Lena nodded, her heart pounding. When the local anesthetic took hold and her gum went numb, she realized the dread had already been worse than anything the drill could do.

The whole thing took thirty-two minutes. Afterward, Dr. Park handed her a mirror. "See? Still your tooth. Just a little stronger now." Lena looked at her reflection and laughed — a small, tired, relieved sound. "I should've done this months ago," she said. "Most people say that," Dr. Park replied, not unkindly. "The hard part is almost never the appointment. It's the walking in."

C Key Expressions · 核心表达

I've been putting it off.
我一直在拖延这件事。
I've been putting it off for weeks, but I really need to schedule that dentist appointment. 我已经拖了好几个星期了,但我真的得预约牙医了。
"put off" = 推迟、拖延。用现在完成进行时强调“从过去持续到现在还在拖”，语气自然，适合解释自己为什么一直没做某件事。
The sooner, the better.
越早越好。
If we have to do this, then the sooner, the better. I don't want to spend the whole week worrying. 反正要做,那就越早越好。我不想整周都悬着心。
"the + 比较级, the + 比较级" 结构，表示“越……越……”。这句在建议别人尽快处理事情时非常地道、简洁。
It's not as bad as I thought.
没我想的那么糟。
The interview wasn't as bad as I thought. I was nervous for nothing. 面试没我想的那么糟。我白紧张了。
"not as... as I thought" 是表达“预想比现实更糟”的常用句型，适合描述做完一件害怕的事之后的释然。
I worked myself up over nothing.
我白白紧张了半天。
I worked myself up over nothing. The test was hard, but I passed it easily. 我白白紧张了半天。考试是挺难,但我轻松过了。
"work oneself up" = 让自己越来越紧张/激动。"over nothing" 表示“根本不值得”。整句带着自嘲和释然后的轻松。
I should've done this sooner.
我早该这么做了。
I should've done this sooner. The relief is immediate, and now I just regret waiting so long. 我早该这么做了。轻松感立竿见影,我现在只后悔拖了这么久。
"should have + 过去分词" 表示“过去本应该做但没做”。这句话是事后后悔时的经典表达，语气真诚。

D Real Conversation · 真实对话

— Saturday morning, a small dental clinic. Lena is sitting in the chair, gripping the armrests. Dr. Park washes her hands —
Dr. Park: (softly) Hi, Lena. I can see you're a little tense. Is this your first time here? (轻声)嗨,Lena。我看你有点紧张。你是第一次来这儿吗?
Lena: (small laugh) First time in... about three years. Maybe four. I've been putting it off. (轻笑)第一次来……大概三年没来了。也许四年。我一直在拖。
Dr. Park: That's more common than you'd think. Most people who walk in here have been putting it off. So — tell me what happened. What made you come in today? 这比你想象的更常见。大多数走进这里的人都在拖。那——跟我说说怎么回事。是什么让你今天来了?
Lena: (pause) I couldn't sleep. The pain woke me up at 4 a.m. And I just... I couldn't lie to myself anymore that it would go away. (停顿)我睡不着。凌晨四点疼醒了。我就……我再也骗不了自己说它会好了。
Dr. Park: Okay. That's honest. Let's take a look. Open for me — just a little. Good. 好。这很诚实。我们看看。帮我张开——一点点。很好。
— Dr. Park taps the tooth gently. Lena flinches —
Lena: Sorry. That one. That's the one. 抱歉。就是那颗。就是那颗疼。
Dr. Park: No need to apologize. Your body is telling us the truth. Let me show you the X-ray. See this dark area? That's a cavity. It's deep, but it's fixable. The sooner we do it, the better. 不用道歉。你的身体在告诉我们真相。我给你看 X 光。看到这块阴影了吗?是蛀牙。很深,但能治。越早处理越好。
Lena: (quieter) Will it hurt? (更轻)会疼吗?
Dr. Park: We'll numb the area first. You'll feel pressure, but not pain. And if anything feels off, you raise your hand. Anytime. You're in control. 我们会先麻醉这个区域。你会觉得有压力,但不疼。如果有什么不对劲,你就举手。任何时候都可以。你说了算。
Lena: (long breath) Okay. Let's just... get it over with. (长舒一口气)好。我们……赶紧弄完吧。
Dr. Park: (smiling) That's the spirit. And Lena? The hard part is almost never the appointment. It's the walking in. (微笑)这就对了。还有 Lena?最难得从来不是看诊本身。是走进来那一步。
— Thirty-two minutes later. Lena touches her cheek, surprised —
Lena: That's it? It wasn't as bad as I thought. 这就完了?没我想的那么糟。
Dr. Park: Most things aren't. Just take care of it from now on, okay? 大部分事情都没那么糟。以后好好照顾它,好吗?
— Lena nods, still touching her cheek, half-laughing with relief —

Task 2 · Share your opinion 表达观点
Why do you think people often put off going to the doctor or dentist? Is it fear, cost, time, or something else? Use at least 3 sentences.
  • I think it's usually a mix of fear and denial. People tell themselves "it's not that bad" because admitting something is wrong means they have to deal with it. And dealing with it might mean pain, money, or just inconvenience. So they wait — until the problem makes the decision for them. 我觉得通常是恐惧加否认。人们跟自己说“没那么糟”,因为承认有问题就意味着要处理。而处理可能意味着疼痛、花钱或者麻烦。所以他们等——等到问题替他们做决定。
  • In my opinion, cost is a huge factor, especially for people without good insurance. But even when it's affordable, fear plays a big role. A bad experience as a child can stay with you for years. I think the best dentists are the ones who understand that fear and explain every step. 在我看来,费用是个很大的因素,尤其对没有好保险的人来说。但即使负担得起,恐惧也很重要。小时候的糟糕经历可以跟随你好多年。我觉得最好的牙医是那些理解这种恐惧、并把每一步都解释清楚的人。
  • I'm convinced it's mostly about losing control. At the dentist, you're lying down, someone is doing things inside your mouth, and you can't really talk. That feeling of helplessness is uncomfortable. That's why I appreciate dentists who say things like "raise your hand if you need a break." 我坚信主要是因为失控感。看牙医时你躺着,有人在你嘴里操作,你几乎没法说话。这种无助感让人不舒服。所以我欣赏那些会说“需要休息就举手”的牙医。

F Useful Vocabulary · 高频词

cavity/ˈkævəti/
n. 蛀牙;龋齿
The dentist pointed to a dark spot on the X-ray and said it was a cavity. 牙医指着 X 光上的一块阴影说那是蛀牙。
drill/drɪl/
n. (牙医用的)钻头;v. 钻
The sound of the drill made her hands tighten on the armrests. 钻头的声音让她双手抓紧了扶手。
numb/nʌm/
adj. 麻木的;失去知觉的
After the injection, her gum went numb and she could only feel pressure. 打针后,她的牙龈麻了,只能感觉到压力。
dread/dred/
v./n. 非常害怕;恐惧
She had spent months dreading the appointment, but it was over quickly. 她几个月来一直害怕这次预约,但很快就结束了。
appointment/əˈpɔɪntmənt/
n. 预约;约定
She finally made a dental appointment after the pain kept her awake all night. 在疼痛让她整晚睡不着之后,她终于预约了牙医。
