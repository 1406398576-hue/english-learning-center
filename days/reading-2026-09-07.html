The Year the Brick Nearly Broke

📅 2026-09-07  ·  主题桶：商业历史（循环 index 5 / 20） ·  词数约 1,020 词  ·  ⏱ 时长：15 min  ·  难度：真实媒体风（CET-6 / IELTS 6.5 友好）
English Article
本篇为原创深度商业评论（非教材体），用词与句式贴近英文财经媒体真实文风；文中公司史实与公开数据（亏损规模、SKU 数量、公园出售、盈利转折年份）均基于 LEGO 2003–2005 年危机与重组的主流公开报道（Bloomberg Businessweek / Fortune / Harvard Business Review / Reuters）。建议先通读，再回看表达与句法解析。

By the early 2000s, the LEGO Group looked less like a toy company and more like a cautionary tale. The Danish family business that had spent decades building one of the most beloved brands on earth was bleeding money. In 2003 its sales fell by roughly thirty percent in a single year, and by some accounts the company was losing around a million dollars a day. Its debts were approaching a billion. The brick — that perfect, interlocking, six-stud rectangle that had survived wars, recessions, and the rise of television — had finally met an enemy it could not click together: its own managers.

The trouble had been years in the making. Afraid that children were abandoning toys for screens, LEGO's leadership had responded the way many incumbents do: by trying to be everywhere at once. The company chased video games, launched clothing lines, opened theme parks, and poured money into one failed product line after another — Galidor, Jack Stone, Clikits, a line of girl-oriented jewelry kits. Executives signed expensive licensing deals for Star Wars and Harry Potter, which briefly made the numbers look healthy while quietly teaching the company to depend on other people's stories rather than its own. Meanwhile the catalogue ballooned: at one point the company was producing more than thirteen thousand distinct elements, many of them used in a single set, never again. LEGO had spread itself so thin that it no longer knew what it was for.

The man who walked into this mess was Jørgen Vig Knudstorp, a former McKinsey consultant who took over as CEO in 2004 at the age of thirty-six — the first non-family member to run the company. His diagnosis was almost embarrassingly simple. LEGO had lost touch with its core. The company had spent years asking what else it could sell, when it should have been asking how to make the brick indispensable again. "We had lost sight of the essence of what LEGO is," he later told interviewers. The recovery plan, in its early phase, was less about dazzling innovation than about subtraction: cutting the number of unique elements by nearly half, killing dozens of product lines, selling the Legoland theme parks to focus on the core business, and slowing the frantic pace of new-product launches.

The deeper move was cultural. Knudstorp did not want LEGO to stop innovating; he wanted innovation to stop being random. The company had been treating every idea as equal and every market as promising. Under the new system, ideas were tested relentlessly — prototypes built, put in front of children, watched through one-way glass — before they were allowed to consume the company's scarce engineering time. "Innovation is a discipline," became a kind of internal motto. The goal was not more products but better ones, built on a shared system of play in which every new set could connect to every other set. A castle from one theme could meet a spaceship from another. The brick, not the fad, was the platform.

The results were slow at first, then startling. LEGO returned to profit in 2005, earlier than most analysts expected, and the recovery proved durable. By 2015 the company had overtaken Mattel to become the world's largest toy maker by revenue, a position built not on a single hit movie or gadget but on the steady compounding of a simple idea executed well. When the company did embrace outside stories — the Star Wars and Harry Potter lines it had once leaned on too heavily — it did so as an addition to the core, not a replacement for it. The movies that followed, including The LEGO Movie, worked because they treated the brick as a character, not a billboard.

There is a temptation to read this story as a hymn to nostalgia, a vindication of "back to basics." That misses the point. LEGO's crisis was never caused by change; it was caused by change without discipline. The company did not stop experimenting, licensing, or expanding into new media. It simply stopped doing all of it at once, and learned to say no to anything that did not strengthen the system at the center. The lesson for any business — or any person juggling too many projects — is uncomfortable in its simplicity: when you try to be everything, you stop being anything. Focus is not the absence of ambition. It is ambition, finally, pointed in one direction.

Nearly two decades later, the story has the clean arc of a fable — crisis, diagnosis, subtraction, return. But the real moral is quieter. The brick nearly broke not because the world changed, but because the people who loved it forgot to ask what made it worth loving. Companies do not fail when they lose their market. They fail when they lose their reason for existing, and mistake motion for progress. LEGO's salvation was not a new strategy. It was remembering the oldest one: build something small, build it well, and let it snap together with everything else you have ever made.

the numbers look healthy 账面数字很好看（实际暗藏问题）

mistake motion for progress 把忙乱误当作进步

商界高频搭配：想法不值钱，执行见高下——execute on / execute well。

The brick — that perfect, interlocking, six-stud rectangle that had survived wars, recessions, and the rise of television — had finally met an enemy it could not click together: its own managers.
句型主语 + 破折号插入语 + 主句 + 冒号同位：插入语用名词短语 + that 定语从句扩充主语信息；冒号后名词短语（its own managers）作 enemy 的同位解释，收尾抖包袱。
逻辑先铺垫积木的"常胜"历史，再转折出真正的敌人不是时代而是管理——反差点制造幽默与观点。
表达click together（拼合）双关：敌人无法被"拼合/理解"；it could not click together 既指物理也指逻辑。
Afraid that children were abandoning toys for screens, LEGO's leadership had responded the way many incumbents do: by trying to be everywhere at once.
句型形容词短语作原因状语开头 + 主句 + 冒号引出方式：Afraid that… 省略 Being，前置表原因；the way many incumbents do 用 the way 引导比较。
逻辑先给动机（恐惧），再给反应（跟风多元化）——把企业的短视行为解释得合情合理，便于读者共情。
表达incumbent（在位者/既有企业）是商业评论高频词；try to be everywhere at once 精确描述"全面铺开"的慌乱。
The company had spent years asking what else it could sell, when it should have been asking how to make the brick indispensable again.
句型had spent… asking + when 对比从句（should have been doing）：两个 ask 的对比句，用 when 连接表达"本该做而没做"的遗憾。
逻辑一句话点出战略错误的核心：问错了问题——从"还能卖什么"转向"如何让核心不可替代"。
表达should have been asking 用完成进行时强调"整个期间都该做却没做"，比 should ask 更有时间厚度。
Innovation is a discipline, became a kind of internal motto.
句型整句引语作主语 + 系动词：把口号整句当作主语（直接引语/引号句子主语化），是新闻写作点题的常见手法。
逻辑用一句内部口号浓缩文化变革——创新不是灵感爆发，而是可训练的纪律。
表达discipline 在此指"纪律/规范化的方法论"，与 creativity 相对照，是管理话语里的经典对仗。
Companies do not fail when they lose their market. They fail when they lose their reason for existing, and mistake motion for progress.
句型否定句 + 肯定句对仗：先破后立，两句同构（do not fail when… / fail when…），节奏铿锵，适合作为全文观点句。
逻辑把失败归因从外部（市场）转向内部（存在意义），并追加 mistake A for B 的第二个错误——忙而不进。
表达mistake A for B（把 A 误当成 B）是高频搭配；motion vs progress 的对照与"忙碌≠进步"的现代职场反思共鸣。
Chinese Summary · 中文导读

核核心内容

文章讲 LEGO 2003–2005 年濒临破产又起死回生的故事：因害怕孩子被屏幕抢走，管理层病急乱投医——做游戏、卖服装、开主题乐园、押注星战与哈利·波特授权，SKU 膨胀到 13,000+，结果 2003 年营收暴跌约三成、一度日亏约百万美元、债务逼近十亿。36 岁的新 CEO Knudstorp（首位非家族掌门人）开出的药方近乎朴素：先做减法——零件种类砍近一半、砍掉几十条产品线、卖掉乐高乐园、放缓上新节奏；再做文化手术——让创新成为纪律，每款新品先经儿童实测再投产，一切围绕"积木系统"这一个核心。2005 年转盈，2015 年营收登顶全球玩具业。作者点题：危机不是源于"变了"，而是源于无纪律地乱变——"当你试图成为一切，你就什么都不是"。

象背后文化现象

① 大公司病：对"被时代抛弃"的恐惧催生跟风式多元化，动作越多离用户越远；② 授权的陷阱：别人的 IP 能撑起账面，却会掏空自己的创造力——"依赖别人的故事，而不是自己的故事"；③ 减法即战略：Knudstorp 式 turnaround 的共同点不是发明新东西，而是敢于砍掉旧东西；④ 复利与长期主义：一个简单想法被持续执行到位，会像滚雪球一样积累出护城河。

思值得思考的问题

· 对你个人同样适用：你正在"多线作战"，还是"围绕一个系统做深"？哪些项目属于该砍的 SKU？
· "忙"与"进步"如何区分？你最近三个月的高强度忙碌，产出了多少可累积的结果？
· 如果你要为自己写一条 internal motto（内部准则），会是什么？

Speaking Practice · 口语讨论
Question: LEGO's crisis came from "trying to be everything at once." Do you think this failure pattern applies to individuals too — to how people juggle study, work, and side projects today? Draw on the article (spread too thin, discipline, subtraction, compounding) and describe one thing you should probably "cut" from your own life right now. Aim for about 200 words.
参考范文 · Model Answer（≈200 words） I think the pattern applies to individuals even more than to companies. Companies at least have accountants to remind them they're bleeding money; people can bleed time for years and never notice. I catch myself doing exactly what LEGO did: I signed up for two online courses, a weekly gym plan, a language exchange, and a side project, all in the same month. Everything felt productive, which is precisely the trap — I was mistaking motion for progress. The article's lesson that hit me hardest is subtraction. Knudstorp didn't save LEGO by inventing a brilliant new toy; he cut thirteen thousand parts down to seven thousand. Cutting feels like failure, so we avoid it. But every new commitment I add is a new SKU — it costs attention even when it sits unused, and it dilutes the system at my center. If I applied the discipline test, most of my side projects would fail it: they don't connect to each other or to my real goal. So the thing I should cut is my second language course. It's the Galidor of my life — shiny, optional, and stealing energy from the one thing I actually want to get good at. Focus, as the article says, is ambition pointed in one direction.
练习建议：先遮住范文，用手机录音说满 200 词；再对照范文，圈出可复用的 3 个表达（如 mistaking motion for progress、cutting feels like failure, so we avoid it、ambition pointed in one direction），下次复用。重点练 spread too thin / subtraction / compounding 三个词的自然嵌入。
