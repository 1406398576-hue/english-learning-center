The Great Aesthetic Flattening: Why Every City Looks the Same

美学大平替：为什么每个城市在你的首页都长一个样？

📅 2026-08-07 🎯 主题桶：人类与社会 (40%) 📏 难度：IELTS R6.5 / 媒体真实文风 ⏱ 时长：约 15 分钟

Today's Topic · 今日主题

⏱ 0:00–2:00 热身

你有没有过这种体验：飞到一座陌生城市，转角那家咖啡馆却像极了你在 Brooklyn 见过的那家；民宿大堂像 Berlin 的共享办公室；连"宝藏小店"都像是同一个 Instagram 账号设计的。过去十年，城市的视觉质感正在悄悄趋同。今天我们读一篇文化观察，聊"美学大平替（aesthetic flattening）"——算法、流量和"出片"逻辑如何把全世界的好地方压成同一套审美模板。这不只关于装修风格，也关于我们这个"看图像多过看世界"的时代。

💡 读前小思考：你最近去过的某家"网红店 / 目的地"，是它真的独特，还是它"长得像所有网红店"？你更想遇到意外，还是想要"不出错的好看"？

English Article · 精读

⏱ 2:00–10:00 精读

A traveler landing in Lisbon, Seoul, or Mexico City for the first time might expect the shock of the unfamiliar. Instead, many report a strange feeling of déjà vu. The café on the corner could be the one in Brooklyn; the hotel lobby echoes a co-working space in Berlin; the "hidden gem" restaurant was, it turns out, styled by the same Instagram account that styled a thousand others. Somewhere in the past decade, the visual texture of cities began to converge.

Call it the great aesthetic flattening. Across the world, a narrow set of looks—raw wood and rattan, terracotta and beige, the carefully messy bookshelf, the neon-free "clean" palette—has become the default setting for how pleasant places present themselves. It is not that local character has vanished. It is that the version of a place we are shown online has been filtered through a single, global taste.

The mechanism is partly algorithmic. Platforms reward content that keeps people watching, and the safest way to keep them watching is to serve more of what already performed. A photo of a sunlit, minimalist café earns engagement; the next café owner, eyeing the metrics, replicates it. Trends stop being discoveries and become templates. The loop is self-reinforcing: the more a look is shown, the more it is copied, the more it is shown again. Recommendation systems do not set out to make the world monotone; they simply optimize for the next scroll, and sameness is the path of least resistance. The feed, built to surface what is popular, quietly erases what is merely different.

An economic logic sits underneath. "Instagrammable" stopped being a joke and became a line item. Developers commission "moments"—a pink staircase, a mirrored hallway—because they know the photo will do the marketing. Cities court the same aesthetic, funding murals and markets that read well in a square frame. Authenticity, oddly, becomes a design brief: places are built to look unbuilt, spontaneous, untouched by commerce, while being entirely the product of it.

Why do we, the audience, keep choosing the same look? Partly comfort. A familiar aesthetic lowers the cost of a new place; you arrive already knowing how to pose, what to order, which corner to photograph. The sameness is a kind of hospitality. It promises that the world is legible, safe, and ready for you—a feeling that, for a generation raised on screens, can feel like home.

Something similar happens to us as travelers. We have grown used to scrolling a destination before we arrive, bookmarking the same ten viewpoints, the same café, the same overlook. The trip becomes a checklist of images to collect rather than a place to be in. What we call "exploring" is often just queuing for the shot everyone else already took.

The cost is harder to see but real. When a place is valued mainly for how it photographs, the unphotogenic parts—the gritty market, the awkward parking lot, the everyday life that does not frame well—get paved over or hidden. Diversity of appearance becomes a quiet casualty. We end up with a world where difference survives mostly as a backdrop for someone else's content, not as something lived in for its own sake. The effect reaches beyond cities: the same flattening reshapes how we decorate our homes, what we cook, even how we mark a birthday—each acquires a template, because the template is what photographs, and what photographs is what gets validated.

There is a deeper irony. The whole apparatus runs on a promise of uniqueness. Travel accounts sell "hidden gems" and "local secrets"; brands promise to help you express individual taste. Yet the pursuit of the distinctive, conducted in public and rewarded by the same metrics, converges on the identical. Everyone ends up performing their individuality through the exact same tools, to the exact same audience, scored by the exact same number. The more fiercely everyone tries to look different, the more the differences blur into one another.

Not everyone is satisfied. A counter-current prizes the imperfect and the specific: the slightly ugly, the handmade, the unoptimized. Some travelers now avoid the "top ten" lists on purpose. A quieter movement treats a place's awkward, unshareable details as the point, not the problem. They go looking for the slightly off—the things algorithms skip because they do not travel well in a thumbnail. It is too early to call it a trend—and trends, as we have seen, have a way of flattening whatever they touch.

Maybe the flattening is not really about cities. It is about a culture that has learned to value the image of an experience over the experience itself, and to trust the consensus of the feed over the evidence of our own eyes. The world has not run out of difference. We have, for now, agreed to look past it—together, and in the same direction.

Sentence Breakdown · 5 个高级句

⏱ 10:00–13:00 拆解

"A traveler landing in Lisbon, Seoul, or Mexico City for the first time might expect the shock of the unfamiliar."

句型：名词 traveler + 现在分词短语 landing… for the first time 作后置定语 + 情态动词 might expect + 宾语。逻辑：先用"初到者本该有的预期"（对陌生的震撼）铺垫，再在下句用 Instead 反转，制造反差。表达：① landing 分词短语一句话交代"初次抵达"，比用 who lands 更紧凑；② the shock of the unfamiliar 把抽象感受名词化，是评论常用写法，可套：the comfort of the familiar。

"It is not that local character has vanished. It is that the version of a place we are shown online has been filtered through a single, global taste."

句型：It is not that… It is that… 平行结构，两句对举；后半句带定语从句 we are shown online + 现在完成时被动 has been filtered through。逻辑：先否定（不是消失了），再点破本质（是被单一全球审美"过滤"过的线上版本），把锅从"地方"转到"平台审美"。表达：① filtered through a single, global taste 把"审美"比作"滤镜"，地道有力；② It is not that… It is that… 是议论文澄清论点的最稳句型，强烈推荐积累。

"The feed, built to surface what is popular, quietly erases what is merely different."

句型：主语 the feed + 过去分词短语 built to surface what is popular 作后置定语 + 谓语 erases + 两个 what 从句作宾语。逻辑：built to surface what is popular（本为凸显热门而生）与 erases what is merely different（却抹掉只是不同的东西）形成目的—结果的讽刺反差。表达：① surface 作动词"使…浮现 / 被看到"，是媒体高频用法（名词转动词）；② 这种"同一主语的初衷 vs 后果"对撞，是英文评论的高级幽默。

"We end up with a world where difference survives mostly as a backdrop for someone else's content, not as something lived in for its own sake."

句型：end up with + 宾语 + where 定语从句；从句内 as…, not as… 平行结构。逻辑：把"差异"降级为"别人内容的背景板"，对比"为自身而被体验"，点出同质化的真正代价。表达：① for its own sake 固定搭配"为了自身 / 本身"；② lived in 把地方拟人化为"被人生活于其中"，比 experienced 更有温度；③ end up with 表"最终落得……的局面"，自带因果收束感。

"The more fiercely everyone tries to look different, the more the differences blur into one another."

句型：The + 比较级…, the + 比较级… 句型（越……越……）。逻辑：用一个悖论收束全篇——越拼命想独特，差异反而越糊；把"求异"与"趋同"拧成同一条绳子。表达：① blur into one another 把"区别消失"写得极活，比 become similar 高级；② fiercely 程度副词强化"拼命"，让悖论更有张力。这个句型可直接套用：The more we optimize, the less we notice.

Chinese Summary · 中文梳理

⏱ 13:00–15:00 输出

核心内容：初到 Lisbon、Seoul、Mexico City 的旅人本该被"陌生"震撼，很多人却 report a strange feeling of déjà vu——转角咖啡馆像 Brooklyn 那家，民宿像 Berlin 的共享办公室，"宝藏餐厅"竟是同一个 Instagram 账号设计的。作者把这种现象命名为 the great aesthetic flattening：全球好地方不约而同采用同一套视觉（原木、藤编、陶土米色、刻意凌乱的书架、无霓虹的"干净"配色），成了 pleasant places 的 the default setting。真正消失的不是 local character，而是我们线上看到的"版本"被 single, global taste 过滤过。机制是算法的：平台奖励 keeps people watching 的内容， safest 的做法就是复制已爆款，于是趋势从"发现"变成"模板"，the feed（本为凸显热门而生）反过来 erases what is merely different。其下还有经济逻辑——"Instagrammable"从玩笑变成 a line item，开发商和 cities 都 court 同一套审美，连 authenticity 都成了 a design brief（地方被设计成"未被设计"的样子）。作者也承认观众为何买账：熟悉审美降低陌生成本，sameness 是一种 hospitality。代价则是 unphotogenic 的部分被 paved over，appearance 的多样性成了 a quiet casualty。最深的讽刺在于：整套机器打着"独特"的旗号，结果越公开求异、越 converge on 同一套，differences 反而 blur into one another。结尾点题：平替其实无关城市，而关乎一种文化——我们学会了重视"体验的图像"胜过体验本身，并信任 the feed 的共识胜过自己的眼睛；世界没耗尽差异，是我们暂时约好一起 in the same direction 地略过它。

背后文化现象：① 算法 homogenization——流量奖励"已验证的好看"，把发现变成模板，差异在信息流里被静默抹除；② "真实感"被工业化——authenticity 成了 design brief，地方被设计成"未被设计"，恰好呼应你「城市观察者」母题里"人人都在表演给人看"的观察；③ 求异的悖论——越公开追逐独特（hidden gems / 个人品味），越被同一套指标拉平，blurs into one another。

值得思考的问题：这和你做「城市观察者」其实同频：在算法让每个地方都"长得能出片"的年代，真正有人味、带缺点、不可复制的角落，反而成了最稀缺也最值钱的素材。我们是真的"变一样了"，还是只是"被展示的版本变一样了"？——若你写城市观察，这套"平替 vs 具体"的张力，本身就是一篇清醒观点的好母题。

Speaking Practice · 口语练

⏱ 13:00–15:00 输出
🗣 Discussion question： The article argues that social media has made cities and cultures "converge" into a single global aesthetic, and that the more we chase uniqueness online, the more uniform we become. Do you think this flattening is mostly a loss—or are there upsides to a more "legible," shareable world? Share your view in about 200 words, with one concrete example from your own experience (travel, a city you know, or your feed).
参考范文（约 200 词，供对照，不是标准答案） I'd say the flattening is mostly a loss, though not a total one. The upside the article names is real: a shared aesthetic makes unfamiliar places feel safe and easy to navigate, and it lets small businesses reach travelers they never could before. A café in a small Portuguese town can fill its tables because someone, somewhere, made its look "legible" to a global feed. But the cost is the part that stays with me. A concrete example: on a trip last year, I skipped the lively night market near my hotel—messy, loud, hard to photograph—to queue at a "beautiful" café I'd seen online. The café was fine. The market, I later realized, was the thing I'd actually remember. We traded the unphotogenic, lived-in texture of a place for a version optimized to be shared, and the shared version is always the same version. So I'd push back on calling it progress. Legibility is convenient, but a world where every city offers the identical "experience" isn't more connected—it's just more alike. The interesting stuff is still there; we've agreed, together, to look past it.
✍️ 写法提示：开头用 I'd say X is mostly a loss, though not a total one 先立中有转，把"不全是坏事"的克制写在前；中间用 A concrete example: 落实题目要求的"one example"，把宏观现象拉回亲身经历（夜市 vs 网红咖啡馆），制造具体反差；结尾用 So I'd push back on calling it progress 制造论点纵深，再用 The interesting stuff is still there 收束——这是母语者议论常用的"先认、再反、后收"辩证写法，比一味反对更有说服力。
