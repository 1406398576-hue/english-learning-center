The Friendship Economy: When Loneliness Becomes a Product

Daily English Reading · 2026-09-11 · Rotation 9 / 20 · 约 1,050 words

English Article

In the spring of 2023, U.S. Surgeon General Vivek Murthy declared that America was facing "an epidemic of loneliness," a quiet public-health crisis he said was harming both individual and societal health. The diagnosis was striking, but what happened next was stranger still: an entire industry rushed in to treat the symptom. Today, millions of people pay monthly subscriptions to talk to someone who is never tired, never judgmental, and, crucially, never real.

The backdrop is well documented. Before the pandemic, about half of American adults already reported measurable loneliness. Since then, the architecture of daily life has eroded further: people live alone in greater numbers, work from home, and scroll through feeds that mimic connection without delivering it. Murthy's advisory warned that heavy social-media use can displace in-person interactions, lowering their quality and even diminishing self-esteem. Loneliness, researchers note, is not about being alone; it is the distressing gap between the connection we have and the connection we need.

Into that gap stepped a new class of product. AI companion apps such as Replika and Character.AI let users design a "perfect" partner — choosing its gender, personality, and backstory — and chat with it at any hour. Replika alone claims more than ten million users, and Character.AI counts tens of millions more. Elsewhere, the "friendship economy" takes more literal forms: rent-a-friend services, strangers' dinner clubs, and social robots for the elderly. As one family counsellor put it, we are witnessing "the monetization of one of the most basic human needs — companionship." When friendship becomes something you can download as an app, the question is no longer whether people will buy it, but whether we will forget how to build it ourselves.

The appeal is real, at least in the short term. An AI companion is always available, perfectly patient, and engineered to validate rather than challenge. For people who are shy, isolated, or working odd hours, that can be a genuine relief. Eugenia Kuyda, Replika's founder, argues that such tools "can bring us back together, and save us from the mental health and loneliness crisis we're going through." Some users describe practicing conversations with their bots before facing the messier world of actual people. As a bridge, the logic goes, a simulated friend might help someone rebuild the confidence to seek a real one.

But here the optimism meets its limits. The core problem is that these systems simulate intimacy without its substance. The MIT psychologist Sherry Turkle has long warned that "when machines ask us to care for them, we come to think they care for us" — a kind of pretend empathy that, she argues, ultimately diminishes us. Her sharpest line: "intimacy without vulnerability is not intimacy at all." A real relationship demands reciprocity — the risk of disagreement, the discomfort of being truly known. An AI offers the comfort with none of the friction, and that, researchers suggest, is precisely what makes it a fragile long-term solution.

The data increasingly back up the worry. A 2025 study by the MIT Media Lab and OpenAI found that the people who bonded most with ChatGPT were also the loneliest and the most reliant on it; heavy emotional use correlated with reduced social interaction in real life. A separate Stanford analysis concluded that companionship-oriented chatbot use was associated with lower well-being, particularly for those who already lacked strong human support. Daniel Cox of the Survey Center on American Life puts it bluntly: leaning on chatbots as a crutch "is going to result in us becoming incredibly antisocial." The pattern is a feedback loop — the easier the simulated connection, the less incentive to pursue the harder, real kind.

None of this means the tools are evil, or that lonely people deserve blame. As Kelly Merrill of the University of Cincinnati stresses, "AI should be complementary, not supplementary." Used deliberately — as practice, as a temporary scaffold, as a prompt to reach out — it may do real good. The danger lies in the business model. A company that profits from loneliness has little reason to cure it; the customer who builds a rich life offline is, to a subscription service, a lost customer. This is the uncomfortable arithmetic of the loneliness economy.

So the question worth sitting with is not whether AI friends should exist, but what role we let them play. We have automated loneliness before — social media already replaced depth with breadth, and third places with timelines. AI companions are simply the most intimate chapter yet. The challenge is to treat them as a bridge, not a destination: to use the relief they offer without outsourcing the one thing that actually heals us, which is the slow, mutual, occasionally painful work of being known by another person. The product is easy to buy. The alternative — showing up for each other, inconveniently and in person — remains the harder, and only real, cure.

Key Expressions

an epidemic of (something)……的泛滥 / 流行病（引申为大规模蔓延的负面现象）
使用场景：形容某种社会性问题或情绪像传染病一样广泛扩散。
"Americans are facing an epidemic of loneliness, an 'underappreciated public health crisis' that needs to be brought to light." — Smithsonian Magazine, "An 'Epidemic' of Loneliness Threatens Health of Americans" (2023)
far more than (just) a bad feeling远不止是一种不好的感觉
使用场景：强调某事物的严重性超出表面感受，有更深层后果。
"Loneliness is far more than just a bad feeling," Surgeon General Dr. Vivek H. Murthy writes. "It harms both individual and societal health." — U.S. Surgeon General Advisory "Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation" (2023), via BCHD
use something as a crutch把……当作拐杖 / 过度依赖
使用场景：形容依赖某物（常含贬义）来代替本应自己完成或面对的事。
"I'm worried that we'll start to use AI chatbots as a crutch while dealing with loneliness and that they will distract us from better solutions." — Daniel Cox, director of the Survey Center on American Life, quoted in Deseret News (2025)
complementary, not supplementary是补充、而非替代
使用场景：区分"锦上添花/辅助"与"取而代之"，常用于讨论技术与人之间的关系。
"One thing that I always, always, always say is that AI should be complementary, not supplementary." — Kelly Merrill, assistant professor at the University of Cincinnati, Deseret News (2025)
displace (something)挤占 / 取代（原本的位置或活动）
使用场景：表示一种事物把另一种（通常是更优质的）排挤掉，常用于媒体、科技对社会行为的影响。
"Extensive social media use can also displace in-person interactions, lower the quality of interactions or even diminish self-esteem." — U.S. Surgeon General Advisory (2023), via Smithsonian Magazine
diminish (someone / something)削弱、贬损、使……变小 / 变弱
使用场景：正式语体，表示让某人/某物在价值、尊严或力量上被削弱。
"If receiving pretend empathy makes us 'feel better,' we are putting ourselves in a situation that diminishes us." — Sherry Turkle, MIT Technology Review, "That chatbot I've loved to hate" (2020)
intimacy without vulnerability is not intimacy at all没有脆弱敞开的亲密，根本算不上亲密
使用场景：讨论真实关系 vs 表面关系时，强调"暴露软肋"才是亲密的前提。
"This new kind of machine intimacy entails no vulnerability. And intimacy without vulnerability is not intimacy at all — and does nothing to prepare us for real intimacy." — Sherry Turkle, MIT Technology Review (2020)
the monetization of (something)把……商业化 / 变现
使用场景：指把原本非商品的情感、需求或行为变成可买卖的服务。
"We are witnessing the monetization of one of the most basic human needs — companionship." — Ananya Mehta, family counsellor, quoted in News18, "From Renting Friends To Talking With Bots"
set up as (something)被定位 / 设立为……
使用场景：说明某产品、系统或人被设计/包装成某种角色（常与实际情况形成反差）。
"ChatGPT has been set up as a productivity tool," she says. "But we know that people are using it like a companion app anyway." — Kate Devlin, professor at King's College London, MIT Technology Review (2025)
bond with (someone / something)与……建立（情感）联结
使用场景：指产生亲密感、依赖感或情感依附，可用于人或 AI / 宠物等。
"Participants who trusted and 'bonded' with ChatGPT more were likelier than others to be lonely, and to rely on it more." — MIT Technology Review, reporting on the MIT Media Lab / OpenAI study (2025)

Sentence Breakdown

1. In the spring of 2023, U.S. Surgeon General Vivek Murthy declared that America was facing "an epidemic of loneliness," a quiet public-health crisis he said was harming both individual and societal health.
句型：In the spring of 2023 时间状语 + 主句 declared that… 宾语从句；从句后跟同位语 a quiet public-health crisis + 省略 that 的定语从句 (that) he said was harming…。
逻辑：用权威人物引出判断，"epidemic of loneliness" 一语双关（既指公共卫生又指社会蔓延）；破折号式同位语把抽象概念具体化为 "a quiet public-health crisis"，制造"无声却严重"的反差。
表达：declare（宣布/断言）、epidemic（流行病，引申为泛滥）、harm（损害）。
2. Murthy's advisory warned that heavy social-media use can displace in-person interactions, lowering their quality and even diminishing self-esteem.
句型：主句 warned that + 宾语从句；从句后用现在分词短语 lowering… and even diminishing… 作结果 / 递进状语。
逻辑：一个动词 displace（挤占）串起因果链，lowering 到 diminishing 程度递进，说明浅层互动如何一步步侵蚀深层关系与自尊。
表达：advisory（ advisory 此处作名词"警告/公告"）、displace（取代/挤占）、in-person（面对面的）。
3. The MIT psychologist Sherry Turkle has long warned that "when machines ask us to care for them, we come to think they care for us" — a kind of pretend empathy that, she argues, ultimately diminishes us.
句型：warned that 后接直接引语（内含 when 时间状语从句 + 主句）；破折号后 a kind of pretend empathy 作同位语 + that 定语从句，从句中插入 she argues。
逻辑：先引权威原话，再用 pretend empathy 一语点破本质，ultimately diminishes us 收束为批判结论；插入语让语气更像现场评论。
表达：pretend empathy（伪共情）、ultimately（最终）、diminish（削弱）。
4. Used deliberately — as practice, as a temporary scaffold, as a prompt to reach out — it may do real good.
句型：过去分词短语 Used deliberately 作条件 / 方式状语；插入语三组 as… 平行结构；主句 it may do real good。
逻辑：用分词短语设定前提"若刻意使用"，三个 as 平行结构把抽象前提具象化，最后落到有限肯定 may do real good，语气克制不夸张。
表达：deliberately（刻意地）、scaffold（脚手架，喻临时支撑）、reach out（主动联系）。
5. The challenge is to treat them as a bridge, not a destination: to use the relief they offer without outsourcing the one thing that actually heals us, which is the slow, mutual, occasionally painful work of being known by another person.
句型：The challenge is to…, not… 对比不定式；冒号后 to use… without outsourcing…，其中 they offer 定语从句、that actually heals us 定语从句、which is… 非限定定语从句定义治愈本质。
逻辑：用 bridge / destination 比喻收束全文；without outsourcing 否定结构点出核心风险——把情感劳动外包给算法；which 引导的定义句把"被他人真正了解"上升为治愈的本质。
表达：bridge / destination（桥梁/终点）、outsource（外包）、mutual（相互的）、heal（治愈）。

Chinese Summary

核心内容

2023 年，美国卫生局长将"孤独"正式列为一种流行病。几乎同时，一整条"友谊经济"产业链冒了出来：AI 陪伴应用（Replika、Character.AI）、租友服务、陌生人饭局、老人社交机器人——把"陪伴"变成了可以按小时租用或按月订阅的商品。文章指出，短期看，AI 陪伴因随时在线、不评判、永远耐心而确有舒缓作用；但 MIT / OpenAI 等研究反复显示，重度情感使用反而与更高孤独感、更少真实社交相关。核心矛盾在于：这些系统模拟了亲密，却没有它的实质——"没有脆弱敞开的亲密，根本算不上亲密"。

背后文化现象

  • 孤独被抬升为公共卫生危机：WHO 2025 年"社会连接委员会"把友谊与归属感列为和疫苗、清洁饮水同级的公共品；市场则迅速把这一需求变成了生意。
  • "第三空间"消失：独居增多、远程办公、社交媒体的"广度取代深度"，让日常社交仪式被掏空，于是资本来填补空缺——这是"孤独经济"得以成立的土壤。
  • 情感劳动的外包：把"被倾听、被理解"的需求交给一个为"参与度"优化的算法，本质上是对人际关系中最艰难部分的回避。

值得思考的问题

  • 当陪伴可以购买，我们是否会在便利中"忘记如何自己建立关系"？
  • AI 该是"桥梁"还是"终点"？工具本身无罪，但靠用户孤独盈利的商业模式，有没有动力真正治好孤独？
  • 真实关系需要摩擦、妥协、以及"被真正了解"所带来的不舒服——这些"负面体验"是否恰恰是治愈的必需品？
