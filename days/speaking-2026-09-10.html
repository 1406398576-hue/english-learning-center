The Day My Calendar Replaced My Memory

日历接管我记忆的那一天
I didn't lose my mind — I just lost the habit of remembering on my own. And then one quiet Tuesday, my phone died, and I couldn't remember a single thing.
📅 Day 44 · 2026-09-10 📏 难度：B1-B2 ⏱ 时长：5 min 📂 科技未来

A Short Story

For about two years, I have not really tried to remember anything. I do not mean I have amnesia. I mean something smaller and, in some ways, more embarrassing: I let my phone remember for me. The dentist is at 4:15. The flight is on the 19th. My sister's birthday is next Thursday — but only because the calendar app sends me a polite little ping, not because I have ever thought about it on my own.

It was a Tuesday, the kind of Tuesday that does not announce itself. I was on the subway, reading something on my phone, when the screen went black. The battery was dead. I laughed at first. Then I tried to remember the time. I could not. I tried to remember which stop was mine. I could not, either. For maybe thirty seconds, I was a stranger inside my own routine.

When I got to the office, the security guard waved at me and asked, with a small smile, "How was the dentist?" I had completely forgotten. I checked in with my mouth — yes, my gums were still numb. So it had happened. I had gone, and I had come back, and I had no memory of either. That was the part that scared me a little.

That night, I did a small experiment. I turned the phone off, opened a notebook, and tried to write down everything I could remember from the day without looking. Three meetings. A coffee that was too hot. A short argument with my partner about a chair. I got maybe half of it, and the details I missed felt strangely important — the color of the sky, the way the barista said my name, the fact that it had started to rain around four.

I do not think I am going to throw away my calendar. Honestly, I cannot. My life would collapse in a week. But I am starting to believe that offloading every small thing is not the same as being efficient. It is more like slowly forgetting that I am a person who can think. And that, I think, is a small loss that no app is going to remind me about.

B Key Expressions

offload something (onto a tool / a person)
把……外包出去（让别的人/工具替你做）
I offload all my appointments onto my phone so I don't have to think. 我把所有的约会都丢给手机，这样我就不用自己记了。
neutral / slightly negative — often used when you stop doing the work yourself, sometimes with a hint of self-criticism.
ping someone
（系统）给某人发个提醒 / 戳一下某人
The app will ping you 10 minutes before the meeting. App 会在开会前 10 分钟给你发个提醒。
originally a tech/network word, now used for any small notification; also means to message someone briefly to get their attention.
go blank
脑子里一片空白 / 突然想不起来
My mind went blank when the interviewer asked me about my weakness. 面试官问我缺点的时候，我脑子里一片空白。
used for sudden memory loss, panic moments, or a screen suddenly turning off — very flexible in real conversation.
a stranger in your own routine
在自己熟悉的日常里像个陌生人
After ten years abroad, I felt like a stranger in my own city. 在海外生活了十年之后，我在自己城市里像个陌生人。
poetic but natural — used when the familiar suddenly feels foreign, often because of stress, jet lag, or over-dependence on something.
the way someone says your name
某人念你名字的方式 / 语气
I still remember the way my grandmother said my name. 我仍然记得我奶奶念我名字的样子。
a soft, reflective phrase — common in essays and good conversation; signals you are paying attention to small human details.

C Real Conversation

— A Tuesday evening, you and your partner are talking about the phone dying earlier —
You: Okay, so... today was kind of weird. My phone died on the subway, and I — I literally could not remember what stop to get off at. 今天有点怪。手机在地铁上没电了，我下哪一站我居然真的不记得了。
Partner: Wait, seriously? You take that line every day. 等等，真的假的？你每天都坐那一趟啊。
You: I know, I know. But I always... I always look at the map on my phone. And the next stop, and the time. So I never actually learn it. I just follow the screen. 我知道我知道。但我每次都是看手机上的地图，下一站、时间什么的。所以我从来都没真的"记"过，就是跟着屏幕走。
Partner: Hmm. But... isn't that just, like, efficient? 嗯……但这不就是……更高效吗？
You: I used to think so. But then I got to the office, and the guard asked how the dentist was. And I had... I had no idea. My mouth was numb, so I knew it happened. But I could not remember a single thing about it. 我以前也这么想。但后来到公司，门卫问我看牙怎么样。我整个人是懵的。嘴巴是麻的，所以我确实去了，但具体细节一点都想不起来。
Partner: Okay that's... actually a little creepy. What did you do? 好吧……这有点瘆人。你怎么办的？
You: I turned the phone off tonight and tried to write down my day from memory. And I — I got maybe half of it. And the stuff I missed... that was the good stuff. Like the color of the sky. The way the barista said my name. 我今晚把手机关了，试着用脑子把今天过一遍。我只记得大概一半。但我漏掉的那些……都是好细节啊。比如天空的颜色，比如那个咖啡师叫我名字的语气。
Partner: ...okay, that's actually kind of sad. Maybe we should — I don't know — have one phone-free evening a week? ……好吧，这确实有点难过。要不我们——我也不晓得——每周有一个晚上不用手机？
You: Mm. Yeah. Maybe. Just... one evening. Let's see if we survive. 嗯，可以啊。就一个晚上。看看我们能不能活下来。

D Speaking Practice

Task 1 · Retell the story 复述故事
Try to retell the story in your own words. Aim for 60–90 seconds. Don't try to be perfect — just keep going.
  • The story is about a person who has been using their phone to remember everything — appointments, even which subway stop to get off at. One day, their phone dies, and they realize they can't remember anything. 故事讲的是一个人一直让手机帮她记所有事——约会、地铁站。有一天手机没电了，她发现自己什么都想不起来了。
  • The worst part is at the office, when someone asks how the dentist visit went, and she has no idea — except her mouth is still numb, so she knows she went. 最难受的是到公司之后，有人问她看牙怎么样，她完全不记得，只有嘴巴还麻着，证明她确实去了。
  • That night, she turns off her phone and tries to write down the day from memory, and she notices she missed all the small, beautiful details — the sky, the way someone said her name. 那天晚上她关掉手机，试着用脑子把今天过一遍，发现所有细微但美好的细节——天空、别人叫她名字的方式——都被她漏掉了。
Task 2 · Share your opinion 表达观点
Do you think we remember less than people did 20 years ago? Why or why not? Give one personal example.
  • I think we do, honestly — I used to know maybe fifteen phone numbers by heart, and now I don't even know my own partner's number without checking. 老实说，我觉得是的。我以前能背下来大概十五个电话号码，现在我连自己伴侣的号码都背不出。
  • But I'm not sure it's a bad thing. Like, my brain has more space for other stuff now — I read more, I notice more feelings. It's just... different. 但我不确定这一定是坏事。我的大脑现在有了更多空间做别的事——读更多书，注意更多情绪。只是……不一样了。
  • Where I draw the line is with people. I don't want to forget the way someone says my name. That's the kind of thing I want to keep for myself, not let an app store for me. 我的底线是人。我不想忘记别人叫我名字的方式。这种事我想自己留着，不想让 App 替我记。
Task 3 · Real-life scenario 真实场景
A friend says: "I don't see the problem. Phones make life easier. Why would anyone go back?" Respond naturally — you can agree, push back, or ask a question.
  • I hear you, and honestly, I'm not going back either. But — can I ask you something? What do you actually remember from yesterday? Like, the small stuff, not the meetings. 我懂你，老实说我自己也不打算退回去。但我能问你个问题吗？昨天你真正记得什么？小细节，不算开会那些。
  • Yeah, they're convenient. I just don't want "convenient" to quietly become "forgetful." There's a line somewhere, and I think a lot of us are already past it. 嗯，确实方便。我只是不想让"方便"悄悄变成"健忘"。那条线就在那儿，我觉得我们很多人已经过了。
  • That's fair. But maybe try this for one evening — turn it off, just one evening, and see what you remember. If nothing else, it's kind of interesting. 你说得也有道理。但你可以试一个晚上——就一个晚上——关掉手机，看看你能记住什么。起码，挺有意思的。
